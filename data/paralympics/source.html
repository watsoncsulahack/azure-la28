Planning the Paralympic Games

The Paralympics are part of the Games, not an afterthought. Paris ran 20 Paralympic competition venues, converted sites between sports, and used a different security workforce scale (~12,000 private agents vs ~23,000 for the Olympics).
LOS ANGELES · LOOKING TOWARD 2028

Venue conversion

  • 01Paris used 20 Paralympic competition venues alongside 41 Olympic competition venues
  • 02Some venues hosted different sports in the Olympic and Paralympic periods — rapid changes to seating, equipment, layouts, tech, and spectator facilities
  • 03Overnight conversions were extremely tight (example: ~17 hours between sports at Bercy Arena)
  • 04LA28 has published a Paralympic venue plan centered on accessibility and athlete experience
  • 05Confirm sports and sites on the official LA28 venues and Paralympic pages

Accessibility across the Games

  • 01Step-free journeys from transit stations to seats, including sidewalks, ramps, shuttles, and drop-offs
  • 02Accessible seating, restrooms, entrances, signage, and evacuation procedures at venues
  • 03Digital platforms (tickets, apps, travel info) need WCAG-style accessibility
  • 04Communication supports: ASL, captions, audio description, assistive listening
  • 05Test venues and transportation with disabled users before public opening
  • 06Accessibility improvements should remain after 2028 — not only for Games time

Read this data carefully

  • City of Los Angeles streets only. Venues in Inglewood, Long Beach, Pasadena, Carson, Anaheim, and other cities have little or no data here.
  • A corner marked as having a ramp is not a check of ADA compliance, slope, or condition.
  • Ramp points come from the city's 2017 sidewalk inventory, traced from 2014 aerial photos.
  • The venue list is the city's unofficial, subject-to-change LA28 list, not the current LA28 Games Plan.
  • The city is building a newer LiDAR sidewalk assessment for LA28 sites. It is not public yet.

Fieldwork questions

  1. Which venue has the most corners without a ramp, and which has the highest share? Would the same fix work for both?
  2. Pick one city LA28 site above and map the walk from the nearest Metro station. Where would a wheelchair user have to detour?
  3. Walk or map Expo Park/USC station to the Coliseum and Pico station to LA Live. How many crossings are there, and does every corner have a ramp?
  4. Inglewood, Long Beach, and Pasadena venues have little data here. Which agency keeps their curb-ramp records, and is it public?
  5. A ramp can exist and still be too steep, cracked, or blocked. What would you check on site before calling a route step-free?

Security & workforce

  • 01Paris mobilized ~12,000 private security agents during the Paralympic Games (vs ~23,000 during the Olympics)
  • 02Security plans must stay accessible for people with disabilities
  • 03Volunteers and staff need disability-awareness and assistance training
  • 04California is developing Olympic and Paralympic public-safety coordination agreements
  • 05Multi-city venues mean security coordination across jurisdictions

Organizer technology map

A teaching map of public systems Paris organizers used for community engagement and accessible spectating. The boxes are separate tools. They connect because each one answers a different question: who is already involved, who to message, where the Paralympics are published, and how a disabled spectator follows the action.

  1. 01

    Record the community

    National para-sport activity

    Before Paris, the French Paralympic and Sports Committee published what clubs and programs were already doing. That kind of report is the community baseline, not a Games-time app.

    Comité Paralympique et Sportif Français

  2. 02

    Know who to reach

    One supporter database

    Paris 2024 kept a single record for people who joined through social media, the website, or a ticket. Surveys then fed sport-specific email and SMS journeys in five languages.

    Salesforce, Paris 2024 story

  3. 03

    Publish the Paralympics

    A public channel

    The International Paralympic Committee used TikTok for match clips, athlete live video, daily highlights, and short explainers of the 22 sports.

    International Paralympic Committee

  4. 04

    Follow the event live

    Access tools at the venue

    Spectators could use audio description in the official app, a low-vision helmet, tactile tablets, captions, and sign language. These sit on top of the channels above.

    sportanddev
